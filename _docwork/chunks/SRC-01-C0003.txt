  Your three questions, answered

  1. Are you going in the right direction? Yes — the QMF shape is sound.
  QMF-as-toolbox (libraries that build applications, never an application
  itself) is the strongest correction of yesterday's session, and four ideas in
  the design are genuinely excellent: one kernel with two plugs (so backtest ≡
  live is enforced by tests, not hoped for); signals that carry no money
  quantity (so a bot cannot blow the account through sizing); content-addressed
  results (so old test results can never silently lie about new definitions);
  and the two-timestamp rule (the one defence against an agent building in
  look-ahead bias at 3am).

  2. Are you missing anything? Yes — three things, and one of them costs you 
  evidence every single day:

  - Nothing is recording. The economic calendar feed only shows the current week
  — past weeks are unbuyable at any price. Your own tick stream exists only if
  something records it. Everything else in the plan can wait; this can't. It's a
  ~150-line script, not a project.
  - The Spotware application isn't submitted. They review applications before
  you're allowed to build against their API, the queue length is unknown, and no
  agent can shorten it. It gates the entire broker side.
  - The map contradicts yesterday's rulings in three places. It still says Exit
  lives inside the confluence (you ruled it's Book territory), still puts the
  fill engine in core QMF (you paused backtesting entirely), and still scopes
  backtesting into the minimal core. You told me not to treat the map as core —
  good, because if the documentation factory read it today, it would document
  the wrong thing.

  3. Where's the leverage — the "slide"? Five locks, and none of them is a 
  build. They're decisions and small artifacts: the nouns and keys (what an
  Order, Fill, Position is, and how results are keyed), the broker contract + 
  its test suite (written before any implementation — the tests define "done"
  for the two hardest modules), one manifest format (build once, seven uses),
  the one-page agent surface (the entire contract between future agents and
  QMF), and the oracle list (for each module, the external thing that proves it
  right — this is what converts "Mubarak must review it" into "a machine gate
  checks it," which is what actually raises your speed).

  The hard truth on "there's really not that much to build"

  You're half right, and the half matters. Right: most code running on your
  machine will be other people's — DuckDB, Polars, TA-Lib, Optuna handle the
  math, and there's not much to invent per module. Wrong: the total is nowhere
  near small. The honest estimate for QMF alone is on the order of 100–150 
  factory-days — and a factory-day is a day of you reviewing and merging, not an
  agent-hour. Your review bandwidth is the schedule, not agent throughput.
  Three pieces can't be sped up by copying because nothing to copy exists
  anywhere: the cTrader adapter, the prop-firm rule schema, and the fill model
  (how simulated trades get realistic prices). That's why the oracle list is the
  lever worth pulling — it's the only lock that raises your ceiling instead of
  just organizing the work.

  Also, three things in the current plan are over-engineered for where you are
  and should be deferred: the full statistical anti-overfitting battery
  (desk-grade machinery before a single strategy exists), Program/Campaign
  machinery (building the container for prop-firm content you explicitly
  deferred), and the DeepSeek-style extensibility plumbing (your registry design
  already covers most of it).

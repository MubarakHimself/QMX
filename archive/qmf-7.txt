  what QMB is and what it does for you, in plain words. Read this; it's your

  ---

  What QMB actually is

  Two things in one install:

  1. The library — the wind tunnel itself: the machinery that replays market
     history against a bot and tells you what would have happened, honestly.
  2. The qmb command — how you and the agents drive the tunnel from a terminal.
     There's also a Python door (same functions inside notebooks) and later an
     MCP door (same functions for your day-to-day Claude).

  One machine, three handles. Nothing exists in only one handle.

  What you can actually do with it

  Test a bot against a Book. qmb backtest mybot --book scalping@2. That one line
  pulls the scalping Book version 2's rules from the registry, compiles them
  with the BMS rules and your data window into one config file, and runs the
  test under those rules. Change the Book, the next run picks it up. Want
  harsher conditions? --conditions stress-spread — more turbulent wind, same
  tunnel.

  Ask "is there even an edge here?" before building anything. The significance
  test runs the bot's entry logic with ordering switched off and answers: is
  this signal genuinely better than chance, with a number. Monte Carlo shuffles
  and perturbs history to answer: is this result luck. Both are one command
  each.

  Optimize at scale. A bot declares its tunable knobs; qmb optimize searches
  them intelligently (real adaptive search — better than what Jesse actually
  ships, which we discovered is random search in a costume) and every single
  trial is recorded. 12–14 runs at once on a sandbox, each in its own process,
  none stepping on another.

  Test every combination. Same bot × five pairs × three timeframes = fifteen
  separate, fully-labeled runs, one command, results side by side.

  Read what happened. Every run writes logs while it goes, and exactly one
  ledger line when it finishes: pass or fail against the Book's own bar,
  unbiased, plus a report artifact — the metrics, the equity curve, the trade
  list, the chart data your UI will later render. Agents read the ledger and
  report JSON directly; you read the rendered report.

  Anywhere. It installs like any package (uv add qmb — the npm feel you wanted),
  the same on your laptop, a sandbox, or a future machine. Jupyter works with
  no server, no Docker. Data downloads from Dukascopy under your own account —
  QMB ships no data, which is what keeps us legal.

  What it deliberately does NOT do yet: claim edge from fills (the honest forex
  fill model — spreads by hour, weekend gaps, financing — is the one thing no
  one on earth has open-sourced; it's our GAP-0048 sitting, and until then every
  fill result is stamped "optimistic"); validate strategies on synthetic data
  (generation ships for stress-testing infrastructure, and the generated files
  are tagged so they can never masquerade as real history); live trading (that's
  the trading node's job — QMB's loop has the socket for it).

## CRITICAL

### R-C1 — AD-37's conflict rule is scope-blind and can suppress a protective flatten
**Attaches to:** AD-37, l.419 (`Conflict rule`), read against AD-36 l.402/404 and AD-37 l.417.

The collapse rule is correctly scoped (`the *same* mechanical command on the *same* enforcement scope`). The conflict rule is not: *"where pending actions would produce **different** commands, the higher rank wins outright and the lower is suppressed — never both, never queued behind."* It carries no scope qualifier and no notion of compatibility. A kill-switch-class `suspend_new` at rank 1 and a kill-line `flatten` at rank 2 are "different commands"; as written the flatten is suppressed and the positions stay open. That is the exact inversion AD-36 exists to prevent, and it fires in the single scenario the kill line was built for.

**Fix:** scope the conflict rule to *the same enforcement scope*, and add a compatibility clause: actions whose effects compose (`suspend_new` + `flatten`, `drain` + `flatten`) both execute; only *mutually exclusive* actions on one scope arbitrate. Add the standing invariant *a higher-ranked action may never reduce the protection a lower-ranked action would have delivered* — rank decides attribution and ordering, never whether protection happens (the spine already states this for the collapse case at l.418; state it for the conflict case too).

### R-C2 — `close_partial` is a V1 exit kind with no command, and a netted shared account cannot execute a Book-scoped flatten
**Attaches to:** AD-33 l.365 (`V1 kinds (CT-23…): close_full, close_partial, tighten_protective_stop`), against AD-27 l.273 (five kinds; `close_position`/`close_all` carry a scope but no quantity) and Deferred l.592 (*"General `amend_order` … and **partial-close command semantics** | Explicit later mint on CT-19"*).

Two consequences, both live today:
1. A Book may legally accept a `close_partial` intent that no venue command can express. One implementer refuses it always (making the kind dead vocabulary); another emulates it with `close_all` + re-place — reopening an unprotected window, the exact sin AD-34 l.376 forbids for protection amendment.
2. AD-29 l.306 explicitly permits several Books on one account, and AD-27's `close_position` scope set is `account | account-binding | instrument-within-binding` with no quantity. Under a `netting` account (CT-18, AD-28 l.288) two Books' positions in one instrument are one venue position, so closing one Book's share **is** a partial close. AD-37 l.420 waves at this (*"`netting | hedging` decides whether an instrument- or Book-scoped flatten is one command or an AD-27 compound command"*) but a compound command is still N whole closes, not a fractional one. The result: on a permitted deployment, the kill line cannot execute — and AD-27 forbids emulating at a wider scope.

**Fix:** either (a) mint quantity-bearing `close_position` in this increment (a partial-close command, scoped to protection use, with the same risk-non-increasing constraint as `amend_protection`), or (b) drop `close_partial` from CT-23's V1 kinds and add an explicit rule: *a Book-scoped or bot-scoped partial exit is `unsupported capability` in V1*. Additionally, state the netting consequence in AD-29 as a **bind-time** check: binding a second Book to an account whose CT-18 position model is `netting` is a `policy rejection` unless the Book declares no Book-scoped force-flat rule.

### R-C3 — AD-40's closed unit-kind vocabulary cannot type its own sizing formula, and non-USD-quoted instruments have no ratified conversion path
**Attaches to:** AD-40 l.465 (the closed vocabulary) and l.466 (`position_quantity`, `original_risk_amount`), against l.463 (USD numeraire) and Deferred l.589.

The vocabulary is `money(currency) | price-delta(instrument) | quantity(unit) | r-multiple | rate(money-per-r) | count | dimensionless-ratio | duration | instant`, "addable never redefined", with *"Every declared variable carries a unit-kind (null is a refusal, never a default)"*. But `original_risk_amount` is defined (l.459) as *"the cost of a full-loss fill at the admitted quantity"* — dimensionally `price-delta(instrument) × quantity(unit) × [money per price-delta per quantity]`. **That third factor — the tick/point value, contract size, or pip value — has no unit-kind in the closed set**, and `dimensionless-ratio` cannot carry it. So the one formula the dimensional law exists to protect cannot be declared under it, and the symbolic checker (l.465) would refuse the spine's own `position_quantity` derivation. `pip value`, `tick value` and `contract size` appear nowhere in the 603-line spine.

Worse, for an instrument whose quote/profit currency is not the account numeraire (USDJPY, XAUJPY on a USD account — normal in the operator's ≈6-broker forex world), that factor **is** an FX rate at admission time. AD-40 bans silent conversion and ratifies no rate source; Deferred l.589 defers the rate source but scopes the refusal only to *"binding a Book to an account in another currency"*. So the ladder is uncomputable for most of the tradeable universe while the deferral appears to cover it.

**Fix:** (1) add `value-factor(instrument)` — money per price-delta per quantity — to the unit-kind vocabulary as a first-class kind, sourced from the AD-9 instrument-metadata record and refusing (`unavailable dependency`) when absent (AD-28 l.293's pip-formula validation already provides the verification hook). (2) State explicitly in AD-40 whether V1 restricts admissible instruments to those whose value factor is natively expressed in the numeraire by the venue, or admits the venue's own conversion field as evidence at a named AD-7 boundary with a declared knowledge time. Either answer closes it; silence does not.

### R-C4 — `Position` (and `Order`) are never minted as shared nouns, yet three risk contracts reference them
**Attaches to:** AD-2 l.52 (*"Shared nouns … are **defined** in `qmf-core` … edge modules never define shared nouns"*), against AD-41 l.475 (CT-29 *"carries: the position reference"*), AD-33 l.366/369, AD-36 l.403, AD-34 l.376 (*"the position or pending-order reference"*).

`Position` never appears as a defined noun anywhere in the spine. AD-2's shared-noun roster is `Venue, Account, Instrument, WriterId, Bar, Tick/Quote, BarSpec`. Yet position identity is shared between `qmf-venue` (CT-20 observations, order state fold), `qmf-risk` (CT-23 intents, CT-29 exit records, CT-30 scopes) and `qmf-registry` — which by AD-2 forces it into `qmf-core`. It isn't there, so two units will mint it two ways: one as the venue's native position id (breaking AD-9's opacity discipline and dying on a broker migration), one as a derived `fp1` over the opening fill (breaking under netting where the venue merges).

This is the root of three other findings: under `netting`, AD-33 l.369's *"the full realized result of a position, in R, credits the Bot that opened it"* has no addressable subject when two bots share one venue position; AD-41's CT-29 *"AD-27 fill references"* cannot say whose fill; AD-29 l.304's per-binding *"virtual ledger"* has no stated reconciliation to the venue position.

**Fix:** mint `Position` (and `OrderRef`) as AD-2 shared nouns in `qmf-core` in this increment, with identity rules for both position models: under `hedging`, position identity is the venue-native position under AD-9 alias discipline; under `netting`, QMX carries a **virtual position** identified by `(binding, Bot identity, instrument, opening-fill fingerprint)` whose sum is reconciled against the venue's netted position by AD-27's reconciliation evidence, with a stated verdict vocabulary for a mismatch.

### R-C5 — Margin, free margin and leverage are absent from the entire increment
**Attaches to:** AD-29 l.307 (bind-time capability check), AD-32 l.351 (registration linters), AD-40 l.466 (sizing ladder), AD-28 l.288 (CT-18 field roster), Deferred l.572-603.

The words `margin`, `free margin` and `leverage` appear **nowhere** in the spine except AD-41 l.480's `venue_liquidation` (*"the venue's own 'stop out' means margin liquidation"*) and the Conventions row. So the increment names margin liquidation as an outcome it must distinguish, and provides no contract that prevents it: no CT-18 margin/leverage capability field, no admission-time free-margin check, no margin-state record kind, no bind-time leverage declaration, and no Deferred row saying it waits for the node sitting. A Book sizing purely in R can admit a position the account cannot margin; a second Book on the same account can consume the free margin the first one's sizing assumed. Two units diverge immediately: one adds a pre-submit margin guard, one does not.

**Fix:** at minimum, decide the seam explicitly this sitting: add `margin model`, `leverage`, `free-margin nativeness` and `margin-call / stop-out level` to AD-28's CT-18 field roster as `measured-at-connection`; add a margin-headroom record to the per-binding state in AD-29 l.304; and either add an admission-time margin refusal to AD-32/AD-40 or add a Deferred row that names the node sitting as its owner and states the accepted consequence. Silence is the one option the rubric forbids.

### R-C6 — CT-23 is the increment's orphan contract: four ADs bind it, three meanings, no defining AD
**Attaches to:** AD-33 l.361/365, AD-31 l.344, AD-39 l.442, AD-37 l.412, AD-40 l.456; against AD-16 l.163's minted-kinds list, which mints CT-22/24/27/28/29/30/31/32 and **not** CT-23.

CT-23 appears as (a) the exit-intent kind vocabulary (AD-33), (b) *"a declared CT-23 input shape"* for cohort-correlation evidence (AD-31), (c) *"CT-23 reading slot"* for SQS (AD-39), (d) an unexplained binding in AD-37 and AD-40. The docs corpus names it `ct-23-risk-evaluation.yaml`, *"Reserved risk evaluation and refusal; caller unassigned"* — i.e. it is the bot→Book door / risk-evaluation contract. AD-33 l.364 leans on it as already-settled (*"the same door pattern the entry intent **already uses**"*) and AD-40 l.461 puts `requested_r` in *"the ratified trade-intent envelope"* — but no AD in the spine states what CT-23's shape is, who owns it, or that the trade-intent envelope is now spine-ratified. The entry intent is the single most load-bearing contract in the risk domain and it is undefined at the altitude that owns it.

**Fix:** mint CT-23 explicitly in AD-33 (or a new sub-clause of AD-29): name it the **risk-evaluation door** carrying entry intents and exit intents as two typed families; state its required fields (`requested_r`, direction, instrument, declared full-loss price per AD-40 l.461, reason code, the resolved execution binding per AD-35 l.391); and either move the cohort-correlation input shape and the SQS reading slot to their own CT ids or state plainly that CT-23 is the Book's single inbound-evidence port with three declared input families.

### R-C7 — AD-29's BMS cardinality and AD-35's paper target collide
**Attaches to:** AD-29 l.302 (*"**one BMS instance per account**, serving **many Books**; a **Book binds exactly one BMS at a time**"*) against AD-35 l.390 (*"One active paper-routing target per live binding"*) and l.392.

The paper target is a demo account. By AD-29 that account has its own BMS instance; by AD-29 the Book already binds exactly one BMS (the live account's). So either the Book now binds two BMS instances — violating a cardinality AD-29 calls *"a deliberate ruling under AD-17, not an assumption"* — or the paper target's account has a BMS nothing binds, in which case nothing accounts for or constrains paper activity, violating the authority order AD-29 quotes verbatim at l.301. Nothing states whether the paper-routed activity is evaluated against the live binding's kill line, budget and exposure, or against the demo account's own. AD-41 l.481 compounds it: a benched seat routes to the paper target while *"the Book stays live"*, so a single Book can have live and paper flows simultaneously under one BMS binding.

**Fix:** rule it explicitly. Recommended shape: the paper target is an **execution binding**, not a risk binding — it carries no BMS of its own, and all accounting, constraint evaluation and journalling for paper-routed activity remain on the live binding's BMS with `role = demo` recorded on every record (which AD-31 l.340/341 already requires). Say so in AD-29 as an exception to "one BMS per account", and add the paper target to the binding tuple at l.304 or state that it is deliberately outside it.

## HIGH

### R-H1 — `realized_r`'s cost basis is undefined, so AD-41's bench predicate cannot be computed from CT-29
**AD-41 l.475/477.** CT-29 carries `realized_pnl` and `realized_r`; the bench counts *"closes whose `realized_r` is strictly negative **after costs**"*. Neither the field nor the predicate enumerates the cost set (commission, financing/admin fee — which AD-8 l.101 explicitly notes may still exist despite swap-free accounts — slippage, spread). If `realized_r` is gross, the bench predicate is uncomputable from the record it is defined over; if net, `r_multiple` is no longer AD-40 l.459's *"realized result ÷ original_risk_amount"* as stated. The divergence bites exactly at the sign boundary the ruling turns on. **Fix:** declare a `cost_components` breakdown on CT-29 (each an AD-7 `Money(numeraire)` with a named source), state that `realized_r` is net of a named closed set, and make the set an identity-bearing declaration so a change is a format-version mint.

### R-H2 — `book_capital` and `loss_floor` are undefined referents in the ratified ladder
**AD-40 l.466.** `loss_runway = book_capital − loss_floor` sits at the top of the sizing ladder with neither term defined. AD-40 l.464 names three capital records (*"virtual ledger equity, broker equity and frozen paper balance"*) without saying which one `book_capital` reads, when it is measured, or whether unrealized P&L on open positions is in or out — a composition question `brief-formulas-stopout.md` flags as open and owned by another cluster (*"whether equity `E` includes unrealized P&L … is not written anywhere … Every rung above it inherits the ambiguity"*). `loss_floor` is likewise undefined and never related to AD-36's kill line, which is described in the same words (*"a per-Book capital floor"*). **Fix:** name `book_capital`'s source record and measurement instant, rule unrealized P&L in or out, and either equate `loss_floor` with the kill line or name the two apart under the spine's own naming discipline.

### R-H3 — The bench threshold's locus contradicts itself across AD-40 and AD-41
**AD-40 l.467 vs AD-41 l.479.** AD-40 puts `bench_consecutive_loss_threshold [count]` *"on the leash"* — i.e. the Book's `leash_grammar` section (AD-30 l.330). AD-41 says *"The bench threshold is **per-bot** and configurable … strategy-family dependent"*. The corpus (old wiki `scalper_breaker_threshold`, owner `COMP-BOOK-SCALPER`) puts it on the Book charter. Book-scoped and bot-scoped thresholds produce different bench events from the same trade stream. **Fix:** rule one locus. If the intent is *Book-declared, per-bot-family keyed*, say exactly that.

### R-H4 — Who supplies `requested_r` is undecided, and one answer re-admits bot self-sizing
**AD-40 l.461/466.** `position_risk_amount = requested_r × r_unit_price` makes `requested_r` the sizing driver, sourced only as *"the ratified trade-intent envelope's `requested_r`"*. If the Bot supplies it, bots size themselves — inverting the authority order AD-29 l.301 quotes verbatim, and contradicting the QML principle recorded in `qml-original-dig.md` (`TradeIntent` *"intentionally omits volume — sizing belongs to the risk authority"*). If the Book supplies it, the intent envelope's field is a Book-side value and CT-23's shape changes. **Fix:** rule it in AD-40 and reflect it in CT-23 (C6): recommended — the Bot may declare an advisory `proposed_r`, the Book resolves the binding `requested_r`, and only the Book-resolved value enters the command record's identity.

### R-H5 — The Book template's section grammar has no home for the increment's own new declarations
**AD-30 l.330/331.** The declared sections are `charter | footprint_requirements | money_rules | admission_bar | leash_grammar | capacity_and_sweep`, plus four named additional fields. The increment then requires a Book to declare, with no stated section: exit policy `ExitLogicRef` per family and permitted bot-intent kinds (AD-33 l.367), protective-stop attachment `required|optional` (AD-33 l.366), each control-action kind's rank (AD-37 l.417), the force-flat rule (AD-37 l.421), enabled window kinds (AD-38 l.428), `window_forced_flat` (AD-38 l.437), the paper routing target and paper starting balance (AD-35 l.390/392), the SQS parameter set (AD-39 l.449), the kill-line value (AD-36 l.402), and the loss-predicate format version (AD-41 l.475). Since AD-32 l.351's layer-1 linter checks *"Completeness against the declared contract format version"*, an undeclared home is an unpassable or arbitrarily-placed field. **Fix:** add the sections this increment actually requires — `exit_policy`, `control_policy`, `protection_windows`, `paper` — as a contract-format-version mint (AD-30 l.330 already licenses a seventh section), and map every new Book-declared field to one.

### R-H6 — The state vocabularies the increment relies on are never enumerated
**AD-40 l.464, AD-41 l.481, AD-36 l.402, Conventions l.526.** The Conventions row correctly rules *"runtime state (Book mode, seat state, order state, bench counts, standing protection intent…) is a read-time fold … never a stored mutable field"* — but only `Book mode` gets values (`LIVE | PAPER`). `roster state — a seat-state enum` (AD-40) has no values, though `.memlog.md` records the operator's round-4 ruling as `bot seat active | benched`. `brief-book-bms.md` additionally ruled a two-list split (Book-binding state never contains `BENCHED`; `ADMITTED` is not a state on either binding) that the spine did not carry. And AD-36 l.402's *"stands the Book down"* introduces a third state word with no vocabulary and no home. **Fix:** enumerate both lists in AD-31 or AD-41 (`seat: active | benched`; `Book binding: <named set including stood-down>`), state that `ADMITTED` is the absence of a binding, and add both to the Conventions naming row.

### R-H7 — AD-24's heavy-by-default gate and AD-39's conservative sentinel deadlock V1
**AD-24 l.233/234 vs AD-39 l.445/450.** AD-24: *"**Until the live-path rung has a recorded baseline, every configuration is heavy by default and a light claim is refused at the gate**"* and *"A heavy configuration's synchronous entry point returns `unsupported capability`"*. AD-39 makes SQS a CT-16 configured producer consulted synchronously by a Book door, and rules *"undefined, unreachable, stale or refused ⇒ **hard block**"*. Composed: until benchmark baselines exist, every SQS read refuses and every entry is blocked. Neither AD names the interaction; one implementer will treat SQS as exempt, another will follow AD-24 and ship a system that cannot trade. **Fix:** state the ordering obligation explicitly in AD-39 (SQS's light claim and its live-path rung baseline are prerequisites to any live binding, alongside AD-32's `not yet ruled` block), or carve SQS out of AD-24's default-heavy rule with a stated justification.

### R-H8 — SQS's conditioning inputs are undefined: session window, instrument class, and baseline statistic
**AD-39 l.446/448.** The ratified formula divides *"the instrument's historical average spread **for that session window**"* by the current live spread. (a) **"Session window" is undefined** and unanchored — AD-8 offers a session schedule on a market-hours calendar and a separate civil-time bucket key (l.105); two implementers will draw "the London session" differently and produce different baselines from identical ticks. (b) **"A hard-block threshold per instrument class"** requires an instrument-class taxonomy that does not exist, and deriving class from a symbol is prohibited by AD-9 — AD-38 l.432 solved the identical problem with dated per-instrument currency-exposure records and AD-39 did not. (c) **"average"** silently picks mean over quantile, which `brief-news-sqs.md` lists as open. **Fix:** anchor the baseline's conditioning key to a named AD-8 calendar identity + session id (identity-bearing, entering the baseline artifact's fingerprint); add a dated per-instrument **class record** as an AD-9 metadata record with a missing-record ⇒ block rule mirroring AD-38; and declare the baseline statistic as fingerprinted contract surface rather than the word "average".

### R-H9 — SQS's score has no declared numeric type and no comparison boundary
**AD-39 l.446/448/449.** Parameters are ruled exact rationals; the *score* — a ratio of two measured spreads — has no declared AD-22 channel kind (`exact-price` / `float-analytic`?), and the threshold and hysteresis comparisons cross an exact/analytic boundary with no declared rounding. Two units comparing `0.5999…` against `0.60` reach opposite verdicts on live money, and the hysteresis band's re-crossing test inherits the same ambiguity. **Fix:** declare the score channel kind and, if analytic, name the AD-22 analytic→exact boundary and rounding mode used at the comparison; or define the score as an exact rational over the two scaled-integer spreads (preferable — it keeps the whole sensor inside AD-7).

### R-H10 — Whether the kill switch may flatten is left ambiguous across three ADs
**AD-36 l.402/404/408 vs AD-37 l.417.** AD-36 assigns flatten authority to (1) the operator and (2) Book policy through pre-declared trigger classes, then *"(3) **Nobody else**"*. The kill switch is neither — it is a global, sensor-fed authority. Yet AD-37 ranks *"(1) protection actions (kill-switch class)"* above forced flats, and AD-36 l.408 sends *"which effect fires at which severity"* to the node, whose matrix can map a kill-switch level to CT-30's `flatten`. So the spine simultaneously forbids and enables a kill-switch flatten. **Fix:** state it directly in AD-36's authority list — either the kill switch is a third named flatten authority (recommended, and consistent with the operator's round-2 "flips the entire thing off"), or its action set is restricted at contract level to `suspend_new | drain` and CT-30 refuses `flatten` from that authority kind.

### R-H11 — `resume` is operator-only, but AD-35 returns Books to live automatically
**AD-36 l.405 vs AD-35 l.393.** AD-36: *"`resume` is operator-only. Escalation automates; de-escalation does not."* AD-35: *"Return to live is automatic only where the clearing cause is itself clocked and mechanical (a seat's next-open bench reset, a day-boundary budget reset)."* Both may be true only if a paper→live transition is not a `resume`, and the spine never says which stream a stand-down and its clearing live on (CT-24 binding transition vs CT-30 control action). Two units will build the auto-return on different streams, producing different journals and different folds for "is this Book live right now". **Fix:** rule that a mode transition (CT-24) and a control action (CT-30) are distinct streams, that a clocked mechanical clear mints a CT-24 transition and never a CT-30 `resume`, and that any control-action-issued stand-down clears only by an operator `resume`.

### R-H12 — Two Books on one command stream have one arbiter and two rank tables
**AD-37 l.415/417 vs AD-29 l.306.** AD-37 defines *"exactly one arbitration point per stream"* keyed to `(VenueId, account)`, while ranks are *"a declared, mandatory, non-defaultable field in **the Book's** control policy"*. AD-29 explicitly permits several Books on one account. Two Books may therefore declare different ranks for the same control-action kind and meet at one arbiter with no merge rule and no cross-Book tie-break. **Fix:** rule the arbiter's rank source — recommended: the arbiter reads the *issuing* Book's declared rank, and where two issuing Books tie, the deterministic tie-break is the Book-definition fingerprint ascending, recorded as a declared non-guarantee of semantic meaning (mirroring AD-8's tie-break framing).

### R-H13 — Which conditions route to paper and which block paper is unclassified
**AD-35 l.388/389 vs AD-38 l.431 and AD-36 l.402.** AD-35: *"Any declared condition that blocks live execution routes the Book's activity to its paper target and evidence keeps flowing; `trigger_kind` is an addable-never-redefined kind, **not a closed enumeration**."* AD-38 and AD-36 then rule that a window and the kill switch stop paper too, so those produce only veto-path decision records, not paper fills. With an open `trigger_kind` set and no classification rule, the next minted trigger has no determined behaviour — and the two evidence products (a paper fill vs a would-have-been decision record) are not interchangeable for the decay sensing AD-35 exists to feed. **Fix:** make the routes-to-paper / blocks-paper disposition a **mandatory declared field on every `trigger_kind`**, and state the invariant that a control which blocks live for *market-risk* reasons blocks paper too, while a control which blocks live for *capital/authority* reasons routes to paper.

### R-H14 — The paper-balance reset channel weakens a four-layer-unanimous prohibition, and an earlier operator ruling is silently superseded
**AD-35 l.392 and l.389.** (a) *"frozen at flip, never hand-adjusted, and **resettable only by a dated operator action**"* — `brief-paper-mode.md` records the no-hand-adjust rule as unanimous across every corpus layer (GitBook *"May never: hand-adjust paper balance"*, DEC-0014, DEC-0023, with a named refusal). "Reset" and "hand-adjust" are distinguishable only if the spine says so, and it does not. (b) AD-38 l.431's *"stops live and paper entries alike"* reverses the operator's dated 2026-08-18 ruling recorded at `tracker/map.md:78` (*"while blocked, bots may continue in paper mode so alpha-decay data keeps flowing"*). The reversal is legitimate — `.memlog.md` records a round-2 operator ruling on 2026-08-20 that a blackout stops all trading including paper — but the spine does not record that the earlier ruling is superseded, so the documentation-factory step will carry two contradictory rulings forward. **Fix:** (a) state that a reset starts a *new* paper run with a new frozen balance and a lineage edge, never an adjustment to a running one, keeping the prohibition intact; (b) add a supersession note (date + ruling) for `map.md:78` in AD-38 or the Inherited-Invariants table.

### R-H15 — A partial entry fill breaks the frozen R
**AD-40 l.459/460 vs AD-27 l.276.** `original_risk_amount` is *"the cost of a full-loss fill at the **admitted quantity**"*, frozen at admission and never re-based. AD-27's order-state fold has `partially-filled*` as a normal state. A 50%-filled entry therefore carries an R denominator twice its real risk: every `r_multiple` on that position is halved, the bench predicate mis-fires, and `seat_r_ceiling` is silently under-consumed. **Fix:** rule it in AD-40 — either the freeze binds to *filled* quantity at the first terminal fill state (with the admission-time value retained as the declared plan), or a partially-filled entry is closed and re-admitted, or the residual is cancelled and R is re-based once with a recorded lineage edge. Any of the three works; the silence does not.

### R-H16 — Protection windows sit outside the cohort key and outside control-evidence tagging
**AD-35 l.394, AD-36 l.407, AD-38 l.438.** AD-35's `cohort_key` includes *"the active-control set (AD-36)"*; AD-36 tags evidence produced under a control with a typed AD-16 edge; AD-38 rules a window is *"a door-class refusal, not a kill-switch level … never routes through the protection funnel"* — so windows are neither in the control set nor covered by the tagging edge. A decay cohort can therefore compare a news-heavy period against a quiet one and read the difference as alpha decay, which is precisely the error AD-35 was built to prevent, and AD-41 l.482's suppression accounting will not show it. **Fix:** add the active *window* set to `cohort_key` alongside the control set, and extend AD-36 l.407's evidence-tagging edge to window-controlled periods (or add the equivalent clause to AD-38).

### R-H17 — The risk domain's writer granularity is never declared

### R-H18 — Treasury / money-boundary events have no record kind and no journal mapping

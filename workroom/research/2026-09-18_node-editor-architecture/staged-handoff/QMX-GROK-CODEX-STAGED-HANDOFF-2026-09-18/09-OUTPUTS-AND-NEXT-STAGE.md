# Deliverables allocated to the staged process

Use installed BMAD file conventions, with a manifest mapping these obligations to actual files. Avoid fragmenting documents simply to meet a filename count. This file replaces the previous single-pass exit contract.

## Stage A — Grok exploration and BMAD architecture candidate

Provide fresh revision/evidence manifest and reconciled audit findings; operator-intent/current-law conflicts; integrated architecture spine; capability/package/workflow/app/session/data/compute/persistence/deployment contracts and owners; stack/compatibility rationale; requirements addendum; migration/regression strategy; defaults/templates; copilot/skill/hook/evaluation design; initial complete journeys and failure coverage; opportunity/UI readiness maps; implementation sequence; and the installed skill's internal review findings.

Then freeze a complete candidate, not a skeletal plan. Required transfer artifacts:
- `ARCHITECTURE-CANDIDATE-MANIFEST.json` with source revisions and content hashes;
- `CODEX-CHALLENGE-HANDOFF.md` with actual attachment paths and launch instructions;
- `CODEX-CHALLENGE-INPUTS.zip` containing the meaningful review inputs and prompt;
- `RESUME-STATE.md` preserving decisions and work state;
- a brief operator message: `AWAITING_CODEX_CHALLENGE`, exact files, next transfer step and genuinely blocking inputs.

Do not issue the final Documentation Factory handoff at this stage. A candidate may record unresolved issues, but cannot conceal them as complete design.

## Stage B — independent Codex challenge

Deliver independent intent-derived scenario baseline, machine-readable catalog, BDD/behavior specifications, high-order failure sequences, coverage and UI-journey mapping, design-specific findings, diagnostic logs with limits, test/mutation plan and Grok reconciliation handoff. Review the pinned candidate. No repo or architecture edits, no approval-by-assertion, no production implementation.

## Stage C — Grok reconciliation and final architecture

Update all Stage A documents coherently; retain review findings and dispositions; verify material design deltas; provide requirements/scenario/contract/test/UI traceability; report ready, limited or blocked status honestly. Keep independent recheck as a scoped transfer for material changes, not repeated general planning.

Issue `DOCUMENTATION-FACTORY-HANDOFF.md` and a final artifact/revision manifest. The prompt should say:

> Use the installed Documentation Factory skill. Read its actual instructions, the exported architecture-session transcript and the final reviewed architecture package at the exact paths listed in this handoff. Re-resolve the repository and preserve concurrent work. Perform the skill's thorough validation and integration process under the operator's actual delegation, including code/document comparison and further research where needed. Integrate technically resolved decisions with accurate status and provenance; reconcile all challenge findings. Do not invent approval, suppress blockers or elevate donor behavior into policy. Group only genuinely consequential unresolved questions at the end. Produce the epics/stories handoff under the existing pipeline. Do not start production implementation or downstream sessions yourself.

Grok must replace references with real paths and states. Documentation Factory remains a separate manual launch. No generic extra operator artifact-by-artifact review is imposed; the user's installed skill decides its process within actual authorization. It may resolve issues or return blockers according to that process.

## Acceptance coverage

The design must account for: current and materially different non-Book systems; a non-trading data/ML outcome; authored and app-use session boundaries; explicit handoffs and context revisions; headless/node/CLI reuse; file/API/stream semantics; composite apps and dependency versions; multi-source data and temporal revisions; multi-broker/account targets; local/server/GPU preflight and recovery; sequential deployment; skill validation and activation; and safe installation/export to another instance without private credentials.

Use these to test enabling architecture, not to demand a full implementation of every sample app. Stable future UI interaction contracts are in scope; final visual screens and live trading are not.

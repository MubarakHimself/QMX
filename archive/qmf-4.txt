  GAP-0031 — the indicator rulebook

  Plain words: an indicator is any computed reading on price — RSI, moving
  average, ATR. QMF needs one rulebook every indicator obeys, so a hundred
  indicators built by different factory agents all behave the same way. This is
  the contract (CT-16) that qmf-indicators is built on.

  The conflict your five-hats review found (X-4): the researcher and the trader
  want opposite things from the same code. Research runs an indicator over a
  year of data in one burst — what matters is how much it can chew at once. Live
  trading feeds it one tick at a time — what matters is how fast each single
  update lands, 40 bots at once. One design can't be optimized for both.

  My recommendation — one rulebook, two modes, one equality law:

  1. Two modes, both first-class. Batch (whole series in, whole series out — the
     research/burst shape) and streaming (a running instance you feed one bar
     at a time — the live shape).
  2. The equality law: the same data through either mode must produce identical
     answers, and that equality is an automatic test that runs at the merge gate
     (AD-4). This is the piece that keeps research results honest about what
     the live bot would actually have seen. Without it the two modes drift apart
     silently — the worst outcome available.
  3. Warm-up is declared, not discovered. An indicator that needs 200 bars
     before its numbers mean anything must say so as part of its contract, and
     that number enters its fingerprint (five-hats R-6 — the future causality
     gate needs it). During warm-up the output is a marked "not ready," never a
     fake number.
  4. Missing data is marked, never guessed. A gap in the input produces a marked
     gap in the output or a typed refusal, per the indicator's declared policy
     — no silent filling.
  5. Failures are typed refusals (AD-11 categories), same as everywhere else in
     QMF.
  6. One streaming instance, one owner (AD-15's one-writer rule), with a cheap
     snapshot/restore so a node restart can re-warm 40 bots' indicators without
     replaying a full day (feeds five-hats T-7).
  7. Two benchmark rungs in the AD-13 ladder — burst throughput and per-tick
     latency — so optimizing one can never silently wreck the other.
  8. Inputs stay pure: the series-in-bulk shape (price arrays + their scale) is
     defined in qmf-core, and qmf-indicators depends on qmf-core only — no new
     dependency edges (closes five-hats D-1 and D-3 for this library). Indicator
     math runs on floats, which AD-7 already permits off the money path; any
     output that flows back into an order passes AD-7's named conversion
     boundary.

  Trade-off I weighed: streaming-only (loop it for research) is simpler but
  leaves research burst speed on the floor; batch-only (recompute the window
  each tick) can't meet a ~1s scalping cadence at 40 bots. Two modes costs more
  build effort — the equality test is what makes that cost safe.

● Here it is again through one concrete example — a single bot trading EURUSD
  using RSI (a common indicator that reads 0–100 and needs 14 bars of history to
  compute).

  The two modes.
  - Batch is the researcher's way: "here are 100,000 candles from last year —
    give me the RSI for all of them at once." One big calculation, done in
    seconds. This is what a genetic search does thousands of times an hour.
  - Streaming is the live bot's way: the market produces one new candle, the bot
    asks "what's RSI now?", and the answer must come back near-instantly
    because 40 bots are asking at the same moment. The indicator keeps a little
    running memory instead of recomputing everything.

  The equality law. Danger: those are two different pieces of code. If they ever
  disagree — even at the 8th decimal — then your research says "this bot would
  have made money" using numbers the live bot never actually sees. The law says:
  feed both the same candles, they must give byte-identical answers, and a
  robot test proves it on every code change. Nobody has to remember to check;
  the merge gate refuses code that breaks it.

  Warm-up. RSI needs 14 candles before its first real number. The rule: the
  indicator must state "I need 14" as part of its official identity — not bury
  it in code. Why you care: your future look-ahead gate (the thing that catches
  cheating strategies) can then automatically check "did this strategy's
  evidence start after the indicator was warm?" During those first 14 candles
  the output is a flag saying "not ready," never a made-up number a bot might
  trade on.

  Missing data. Broker feed drops for 2 minutes → those candles never arrived.
  The indicator must not quietly pretend nothing happened. It either marks its
  output "gap here" or refuses in the standard typed way — its choice is
  declared up front, so every indicator handles gaps the same declared way
  instead of 100 private behaviors.

  One owner + snapshots. A streaming RSI has running memory. Rule: exactly one
  feeder per instance (no two bots pushing candles into the same one — that
  corrupts the memory silently). And the memory can be saved/restored, so when
  the VPS restarts you reload 40 bots' indicators in seconds instead of
  replaying the whole day.

  Two speed benchmarks. The factory measures "candles chewed per second in a
  burst" and "microseconds per single live update" as two separate numbers.
  Otherwise an agent making research faster can accidentally make live trading
  slower and no gate notices.

  Inputs stay pure. qmf-indicators is a pure calculator: numbers in, numbers
  out. It doesn't reach into your data store itself — the app hands it the
  candles. That keeps it dependency-free, easy to test, and safe to run anywhere
  (sandbox, laptop, VPS).

  That's the whole thing. The "streaming-only" alternative would delete the
  batch mode and make research loop candle-by-candle — simpler to build, but
  your genetic searches would crawl.

  one running instance, fed once by the tick stream, read by all 40. Two

  distinct configurations, not with bot count. And each instance's memory is
  small by design — an RSI holds a few dozen numbers, kilobytes — with the size
  bound declared and benchmarked (that's the GAP-0033 light rule, next gap).
  That's the scaling economics protected.

  (structured logs, exportable metrics, health checks), with full design landing
  at the ops sitting. Two different tools, both already in the spine. Nothing
  new to decide.

  Synthetic indicators from genetic algorithms — yes, possible, and this 
  protocol is what keeps it from getting messy. An indicator configuration is
  just data (formula + parameters), so a GA can mint thousands of candidates and
  run them in batch mode, each with its own fingerprint. Even a novel generated
  formula just has to obey the same rulebook (declare its warm-up, mark its
  gaps) before its results enter governed evidence. Outside governed evidence
  you can experiment with anything in plain Python — the don't-box-in ruling

  Equality law + "by the time it's live there's zero error" — your instinct and
  the law are the same thing. The equality test is the machine that makes "zero

● GAP-0032 — which math library is "the truth"

  Plain words: when two computers compute RSI and get slightly different
  numbers, something must be the referee. This gap picks the referee and pins
  its exact version, so "RSI(14)" means one thing everywhere, forever.

  Recommendation — pin TA-Lib 0.7.1, upgrades never silent:

  1. The referee is TA-Lib — the industry-standard indicator library (150+
     indicators, 25 years old). Verified on the web today: current version is
     0.7.1 for both its core C library (released 2026-07-03) and its Python
     wrapper (2026-07-16), with official wheels for our pinned Python 3.14 on
     Windows + Linux. That exact version pair gets pinned per QMF release.
  2. Batch mode wraps TA-Lib directly — your existing law already says wrap
     established formulas, never rewrite them just to own the math. Streaming 
     mode is QMX-owned code where TA-Lib has no streaming form — and the
     GAP-0031 equality law you just ratified is what proves the streaming code
     gives the exact same numbers as the TA-Lib reference. The two gaps lock
     together.
  3. Upgrades are a gated event, never automatic. When a new TA-Lib version
     appears, the factory runs a comparison suite first. If any indicator's
     numbers change, that's a formal contract version change with recorded
     before/after evidence — never a silent drift. Live proof this matters:
     version 0.7.1 itself changed the output of four indicators (MACD among
     them) in certain configurations. Anyone who upgraded blindly got different
     backtest results with no record of why.
  4. Every comparison check is recorded evidence: input fingerprint + parameter
     fingerprint + declared tolerance + verdict, registered like everything else
     — so "our numbers match the reference" is a checkable fact, not a claim.
  5. Tolerances are declared per indicator: exact match required where possible;
     a stated tiny float tolerance where cross-machine float wobble is expected
     (the spine already says float bit-identity across OSes is never promised).

  GAP-0034 — the first market-structure families

  Plain words: qmf-structure turns raw candles into the things you actually
  trade off — levels, zones, breaks of structure. The danger this gap exists to
  kill is look-ahead: a support level that looks obvious on a chart after the 
  fact wasn't knowable at the time — a backtest using it is quietly cheating.
  Your spine already has the cure (AD-8: causality lives on instants); this gap
  applies it.

  Recommendation — ratify the lifecycle law now, pick the families from your 
  brain-dump:

  The law (every family obeys it, forever):
  1. Every structure output carries two times: observed-at (when the pattern
     first appeared) and confirmed-at (when its rule was definitively met). A
     third, invalidated-at, is appended when it stops holding — never deleted,
     history stays.
  2. A family may only ship if its confirmation rule can be stated precisely as
     "confirmed the moment X happens" — where X is knowable at that instant.
     Example: a swing high is observed at a peak candle but only confirmed two
     candles later, when both neighbors have closed lower. Any family whose rule
     can't be stated that precisely is postponed — exactly what your gap report
     recommends.
  3. Evidence discipline: results consumed as evidence use confirmed-at. Acting
     on a not-yet-confirmed pattern is allowed (scalpers do), but the output is
     labeled unconfirmed — a different evidence class, never silently mixed with
     confirmed.
  4. The confirmation delay (those "2 candles later") is declared and
     fingerprinted, like indicator warm-up — the researcher's data-split
     protections need that number (five-hats R-5).
  5. Families are QMX-owned, versioned, composable per your multiplicity ruling
     (AD-17: a composite level is its own artifact with lineage to its
     children).

  Seed families I'd start with (the classic ones whose causal rules state
  cleanly): swing points (the atom everything else builds on) → horizontal
  levels from confirmed swings → zones (price bands around levels) → structure
  breaks (price closing through a confirmed level/swing). But you're the trader
  — the gap says start with the smallest operator-used set, and the corpus never
  recorded which level/zone/structure concepts your confluences actually use.

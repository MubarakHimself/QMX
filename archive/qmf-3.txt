  GAP-0007 — How money is represented

  The problem: computers' normal decimal numbers are approximations. 0.1 + 0.2
  doesn't equal 0.3 exactly. In a trading system that must produce
  byte-identical, fingerprintable results, money can never ride on
  approximations. The docs already ban that; what's open is what we use instead.

  My proposal: store every amount as a whole number of the smallest unit — the
  way you'd count in cents instead of dollars, or in tenths-of-a-pip for FX
  prices. Each value carries its currency and how many decimal places that
  currency uses. A small wrapper type displays it normally ("1,250.50 USD") so
  humans and agents read it naturally.

  - Arithmetic is exact — no drift, ever.
  - The same value is the same bytes on every machine, which is what makes
    fingerprinting and content-addressed results trustworthy (feeds GAP-0010
    directly).
  - Rounding is only allowed at named boundaries (sending an order to the venue,
    accounting entries), never silently mid-calculation.
  - This covers three siblings with one discipline: Money (amounts), Price,
    Quantity.

  The realistic alternative is Python's built-in Decimal type — also exact and
  slightly more familiar-looking in code, but slower and it needs extra
  normalization rules so the same value always hashes identically (1.10 vs 1.1
  are different bytes to it). Trading systems that fingerprint their results
  overwhelmingly use whole-unit integers.

● Now the time-audit verdict, in plain words.

  GAP-0008 closed out — what the two agents found

  Bottom line: your time model survived. Nothing contradicts any law or past
  ruling — in fact it matches what the docs corpus itself was already leaning
  toward. But the audit found the model as I first stated it was incomplete:
  five missing pieces would have forced reopening the core later — exactly the
  rebuild-pressure you're designing against. Both full reports are saved in the
  planning folder; here's what they change, in plain words:

  1. The core language gets two more time words: a length of time ("15 minutes")
     and a time window ("from 13:30 to 13:45"). News blackouts, sessions,
     cooldowns, hold limits — all of them are windows; without a shared type,
     each component would invent its own incompatible one.
  2. Two kinds of clock, kept apart by type: the wall clock says when something
     happened (stored forever, UTC); a stopwatch measures how long things take
     (never stored as a time). Mixing them is a classic trading-system bug — an
     internet clock correction mid-measurement produces negative latencies.
  3. Ties are real: most price feeds are only millisecond-accurate, so two ticks
     "at the same instant" are routine. Order is decided by the timestamp plus 
     a running counter — a timestamp alone is never used to order events or as a
     record's key.
  4. Calendars are venue-specific plug-in packages with versions, and the
     calendar version becomes part of every result's fingerprint. Why: the
     world's timezone rulebook gets updated several times a year; without this,
     an update could silently change what "trading day" an old result belonged
     to.
  5. Backtest time and real time are different types, and every stored record
     says which world it came from (live / replay / simulated). Simulated data
     becomes structurally unable to contaminate live evidence — a rule you've
     already ratified in spirit.

  Two trims the audit justified: swap-Wednesday is dropped from the V1 calendar
  (it needs settlement-date machinery, and your accounts are swap-free anyway —
  it earns nothing), and holidays are explicitly in scope for the forex calendar
  instead of forgotten. Plus a book of operational rules (clock discipline on
  the VPS, "never trade before the clock is confirmed synced," gap records when
  the clock was suspect, prop-firm day-boundaries evaluated in the prop firm's
  timezone) — those go into the spine as stated rules for the node and ops docs
  to obey later.

● The sweep you ordered earned its keep. The reviewers found the individual
  rulings sound but caught five places where two of our own rulings collided —
  all now fixed in the spine. Here they are in plain words, for your veto:

  1. The clock rules argued with themselves. One line said stopwatch readings
     are never stored; another required storing one on every broker event (it's
     the only way to catch clock jumps). Fix: stopwatch readings may be stored,
     but only as clearly-marked diagnostic values that can never be mistaken
     for, or compared as, times.
  2. "Journal" meant two things. Evidence journals need the exact nanosecond
     numbers; my observability rule said journals use human-readable text dates
     — which would have destroyed the nanoseconds. Fix: journals are evidence
     (exact numbers); logs are for humans (readable dates). Never the same
     thing.
  3. The prop-firm trap. A trading date didn't remember which day-boundary made
     it — so a prop firm's "daily loss day" and your broker's trading day could
     look equal while meaning different 24-hour spans. That's the value that
     gates your biggest money risk. Fix: every trading date carries its
     calendar's identity; comparing across different calendars is refused, not
     fudged; and a small third calendar kind exists for account-scoped day
     boundaries (holds the prop-firm seam without modeling any firm).
  4. The result label was ambiguous about which version number it carries —
     under lockstep, a bugfix in one package would have re-labeled every other
     package's results. Fix: labels carry the data-format version only; package
     versions never enter identity.
  5. Money was less protected than time. cTrader uses three different decimal 
     scales; my money rule had one "precision" tag with no referent — a yen
     price crossing between packages could be silently off by 100×. Fix: every
     value states its scale; mismatched scales convert losslessly or refuse; and
     broker money numbers are preserved verbatim like broker timestamps, so a
     disputed fill can always be reconciled.

  Also fixed without needing you: the fingerprint recipe is now fully pinned

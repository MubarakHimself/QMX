  Now the real answer to your brain dump.

  You changed my mind — halfway

  My revised position: QML comes first, but as a kernel, not as a full library.
  Here's the reasoning, both directions:

  Where you're right. I told you libraries get extracted from working systems,
  and React proves it — but your situation genuinely differs in one way: you
  already have two consumers on day one. Live bots and your experimentation
  environments (genetic algorithms over indicators, random forests over
  strategies) will both need the same market data access, the same indicators,
  the same exit mechanisms. When two consumers exist from the start, shared
  components aren't premature abstraction — they're just correct. And the
  Cordiverse paper gives you a real organizing principle, not a vague wish:
  everything is a plugin, plugging in declares what it needs, unplugging cleanly
  undoes everything it did. If the trading node is built without that
  convention and QML arrives later, you'd rebuild the node to fit it. That's
  your instinct, and it's sound.

  Where I hold the line — two hard truths:

  1. "QML spans the entire platform" is how this project died before. A library
  that owns bots + agents + UI + adapters for everything is a bigger project
  than the trading node itself, and it has to be built in your least-experienced
  territory (framework design) before anything trades. The escape: QML V1 = the
  kernel (the plugin/context/lifecycle machinery from the paper, in Python,
  kept small) + the conventions every component follows + only the components
  with two real consumers today (market data, indicators, strategy surface). UI,
  agents, and the rest become sibling packages later that follow the same 
  conventions — an ecosystem like Cordiverse itself (many packages, one
  paradigm), not one giant library. You get uniformity without the mega-project.
  2. Revertible effects stop at the broker. The paper's "unplug and undo
  everything" applies to internal wiring — registrations, subscriptions,
  services. A filled order at cTrader cannot be reverted by unloading a plugin.
  So the live money path must be composed at startup, journaled append-only, and
  fail-closed — hot-swapping stays for experiments, paper contexts, and the
  agentic layer. This isn't old-QMX nostalgia; it's what keeps a composable
  platform from silently un-accounting real money. The plugin paradigm and a
  deterministic money path are compatible — the paradigm governs how components 
  assemble, determinism governs what the assembled money path does.

  So the build order becomes: QML kernel + conventions → trading-node concepts 
  expressed in those conventions → components graduate into QML as they prove 
  out. Neither of our original positions — better than both.

  - Where extensibility lives — the recommended shape: one mechanism in the QMF
  libraries, three tiers of composition. Agent workspaces are fully open —
  agents extend freely, and they're safe not by restriction but by absence: no
  live-trading provider even exists in that environment. The app tier is

● The harness study is done — reference\06-deepseek-harness-study.md — and it
  came back with something better than expected: your extensibility instinct and
  QMF's existing design already fit together, and the gap is only three small
  primitives. In plain words:

  - The whole DeepSeek/Cordis model reduces to one load-bearing rule: every
  registration is a reversible effect — anything an extension adds can be
  cleanly torn out. Hot reload and safe swapping are just consequences of that
  rule.
  - Two of their mechanics are gold for money software: the monotonic guard — a
  deny-only registry where a later extension can never undo an earlier refusal,
  so no load order can make the system less safe — and fail-closed approval,
  which refuses before anything gets a chance to answer.
  - What QMF already planned covers most of it: your versioned registry,
  manifests, and fingerprints sit in exactly the slots their system uses — and
  are stronger, being content-addressed. The genuinely new build is seven items,
  of which three belong in qmf-core: the Context (the container extensions plug
  into), the disposer discipline (everything undoable), and a typed event bus.
  - Where extensibility lives — the recommended shape: one mechanism in the QMF
  libraries, three tiers of composition. Agent workspaces are fully open —
  agents extend freely, and they're safe not by restriction but by absence: no
  live-trading provider even exists in that environment. The app tier is
  reviewed. The live node is composed once at boot, fingerprinted, then sealed —
  and promotion from tier to tier is only ever a human edit. That gives you
  "agents experiment like quants experiment with Python" without ever bending
  the live-money rule.
  - Explicitly refused imports: hot-module-reload, auto-reload when a service
  vanishes, and install-time code execution — all fine for a chat harness, all
  wrong for money.
